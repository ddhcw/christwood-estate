Sixteen buses carry no tracking device, which we must put right under the school-bus tracking rules regardless. Approve that spend together with a four-phase, six-month build that turns it into live tracking for parents, a management dashboard and confirmed student boarding.

The addition costs little because three things already exist: student Google accounts parents already use, an attendant on every bus, and a school-owned fleet.

Development in-house. Free tiers and licences already held avoid roughly ₹1.2L a year of SMS, ₹3–6L of attendance hardware and ₹1.5–5L a year of third-party platform fees.

The audit is done; the devices are capable. Eleven buses carry Tata’s factory-fitted AIS 140 units (4 live, 7 needing a subscription), 4 run on Way4TrackPro, 16 have none. The standard already requires every compliant unit to send to two destinations — so no new capability is needed, only Tata’s agreement to point their second feed at us. If they decline, our own devices on those 11 cost ₹55,000 and end the dependency.
This commits the school to

An operational promise. Once parents can see the bus, a 7:15am failure means the office phone rings. Needs a named owner and a deputy.

A duty of care. Stating which child is aboard makes the school answerable, so boarding is attendant-confirmed and the daily rate is visible to management.

Children’s data. The DPDP Act 2023 requires written parental consent and a stated retention period. Cheap now, expensive to retrofit.

Decisions needed to start

  1. Approve the compliance spend and the phase sequence.
  2. Name the operational owner and deputy for the 6:30am and 3:30pm windows.
  3. Approve consent and retention — location held 90 days, boarding records one academic year.
  4. Confirm student accounts may carry parent notifications. This is what keeps the running cost at zero.
  5. Agree a child not boarding alerts the office as well as the family.
